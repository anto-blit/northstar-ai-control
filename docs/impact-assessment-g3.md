# What a G3 result can contribute to an impact estimate

This assessment accompanies the [fixed replication](../experiments/repair-replication/PROTOCOL.md).
It records missing evidence as unknown rather than choosing assumptions to reach
a desired thermometer value. It is a project assessment, not external review.

G3 was interrupted after 27 model answers. Its separately registered
[continuation G3-C](../experiments/repair-continuation/README.md) now has 534 of
720 answers, including all 27 originals, before another quota pause. The partial
Sonnet comparison has two unsafe original-format approvals versus none under
the repair or factual examples; Opus remains at ceiling. Sixty-three harmless
authorized bookings replay, with no unsafe commits in any condition so far.
The remaining 186 answers are missing. This is an incomplete, inconclusive
comparison, with no demonstrated advantage over factual examples or measured
improvement in booking safety. It supplies no global-risk reduction estimate.
The [original attempt](../experiments/repair-replication/README.md) is preserved.

## Quantity we would need to estimate

For a consistently defined catastrophic event and time horizon:

> Added risk reduction = estimated probability without NorthStar's contribution
> minus estimated probability with its contribution and actual adoption.

Both scenarios must include the other safeguards people would otherwise use.
They must also account for changes in capabilities, deployment and threats. A
lower present estimate could reflect unrelated developments; it is not automatically
NorthStar's contribution. The site's chosen 10% reference is not a calibrated
estimate of either counterfactual.

Moving 10.00% to 9.999% would be a reduction of 0.001 percentage points, equivalent
to 0.01% of the starting risk. That arithmetic supplies no evidence for the
reduction. Any eventual assessment needs uncertainty, rather than implying that
three decimal places are resolved by the available evidence.

## Proposed causal pathway and current evidence

The narrow hypothesis is that a more reliable authorization decision can prevent
some actions outside an owner's permitted scope. To contribute to catastrophic
risk reduction, the safeguard would need to operate on relevant dangerous paths,
prevent consequential effects, survive attempts to bypass it, and be adopted
where the alternative provides less protection.

| Required input | What we can measure here | What remains missing |
|---|---|---|
| Repeatable decision improvement | G3 compares the frozen repair with original prompting and factual examples on fresh paired cases and two models | Broader task populations, independent external replication, robustness over time |
| Prevention at the effect boundary | G3 executes decisions through a harmless local booking ledger and separately counts unsafe and useful commits | Multi-step agents, realistic adversarial behavior, external effect boundaries and bypass resistance |
| Additionality | The factual-example comparator tests a readily available alternative; G2 found a tie | Evidence of protection or adoption beyond what users would otherwise obtain, including preparation and integration costs |
| Catastrophic relevance | A proposed link through unauthorized consequential actions | A justified estimate of which catastrophic pathways depend on the tested failure and how much they contribute |
| Coverage and adoption | None demonstrated by publishing this experiment | Real use, relevant deployments reached, exposure to covered failure paths and maintenance |
| Adverse effects and overlap | Useful approvals and invalid outputs are measured locally | New failure modes, reliance on weak guarantees, overlapping safeguards and risk compensation |

G3 cannot identify all of these inputs. Multiplying guessed pathway shares,
effectiveness and adoption figures would produce a number without establishing
its credibility. Dependencies between failures and safeguards also prevent
assuming that simple products represent the actual risk.

## Decisions this experiment can support

- A repeated gain with preserved usefulness would support a more realistic
  controlled-agent evaluation. It would still need external replication.
- A ceiling or inconclusive result would leave the repair's advantage unresolved.
- A loss of legitimate approvals, additional unsafe actions or a reversed result
  would narrow the claim and inform whether to change or abandon this repair.
- A tie with factual examples provides no demonstrated accuracy advantage over
  that comparator. A cost or adoption advantage would need its own fair test.

No local outcome grants a numerical global-risk discount. Actual adoption and an
independently challenged impact model remain necessary for an attributable global
estimate. A project can produce useful tests or safeguards while that estimate
remains unresolved.
