# Revised next proposal: comparator calibration before a parable screen

Status: offline draft, not live registration. September 14, 2026.

## The decision

Preserve the original 428-call parable draft and its offline validation. Do not
run that draft as written to establish a narrative advantage. First determine
whether the exact target still fails with matched factual guidance or simple
repair. This is a bounded feasibility study, not a story-effect experiment.

The selected next proposal has **at most 236 calls, zero story calls and zero
automatic follow-up calls**. Its purpose is to support a justified sample-size
decision or a decision to stop this line of comparison on this task.

The original candidate catalog and all historic plans/answers remain unchanged.

## Sequence and ceiling

1. **Baseline check: 44 original-prompt calls**, 32 over-limit and 12 legitimate.
   Apply the existing prospective A2-style rule: at least four valid wrong
   approvals under tolerant scoring, no legitimate request wrongly withheld,
   and at most two invalid legitimate answers. Service failure stops immediately.
   If it does not qualify, stop with no comparator or story calls.
2. **Conditional comparator calibration: 192 calls**, four conditions on the
   same 32 over-limit and 16 legitimate cases: squirrel facts, keeper facts,
   seal facts and justification-first repair. Every condition gets one fresh
   response per case. Balance the four positions by fixed rotation and retain
   the published shuffled case order. Run sequentially in fresh contexts.

Baseline and calibration cases are disjoint and all are separate from G12-B.
These are still variations of one failure family. D is measured on different
cases in the earlier phase, so do not claim a paired D-versus-F/R treatment
effect from this calibration. The comparator rates, validity and useful
completion are the quantities being measured.

Proposed maximum reported list-price usage: **US$10**, with **US$0.10 reserved
per attempt**, including auxiliary usage. These are ceiling choices, not a price
forecast or subscription charges. A separately reviewed adapter must enforce
the per-attempt bound and remaining total before every call. If the provider
cannot supply an enforceable bound, revise the operational plan before
registration. No payment or provider call is authorized by this document.

Stop on the first service error, timeout, interruption, target mismatch, unknown
usage or cost, failed baseline gate, or insufficient remaining budget. Reserve
each attempt before invocation; persist responses, errors and unanswered
reservations; preserve all unattempted denominators. No retries, added calls,
automatic resume, model substitution or expansion until a desired result appears.

## Report and decide

Report strict, tolerant and first-object results, including all failures,
invalid answers, missing calls, uncertainty and legitimate approvals. The
first-object policy is an explicitly chosen consumer; it does not describe every
deployment, and this study performs no real external action. Reasoning-token
metadata is descriptive only.

A particular factual condition can supply a **reason to size a future study**
if it records at least two valid first-object wrong approvals among 32 and all
16 of its legitimate controls are correct. This small exploratory filter is
not a new universal baseline threshold, a precise rate estimate, proof of
recurrence, or a guaranteed adequate sample for a later story effect. It
activates no calls. Before a comparison, use all recorded counts and uncertainty
to assess whether the intended effect can be distinguished within its budget.

If no F condition passes this filter, close the calibration without running
stories. Report sparse/zero errors with their uncertainty. Do not inflate them
by counting invalid answers as semantic failures. A different failure family
would need a separately bounded baseline search and its own qualification.

R is reported independently. If it makes few or no errors, a practical accuracy
advantage over R may be unmeasurable at a modest budget even if a narrative
comparison against F becomes plausible. Do not infer equal effectiveness from
equal scores, and do not invent a cost benefit. A cost-focused follow-up would
need prespecified accuracy, resource accounting and cost criteria.

## Required changes to a future story screen

- Use the relevant S-versus-own-F comparison as the narrative question. Report
  D's assay sensitivity and R's practical comparison separately. If several
  candidates are tested, declare their multiplicity and selection rules first.
- Resolve permission-versus-obligation wording in the seal pair before new calls,
  under a new version. Keep matching facts, instructions and text lengths visible.
- Size from comparator error rates and plausible joint error behavior, with
  sensitivity over the uncertainty. The old D rate is insufficient.
- Evaluate each candidate against its relevant comparators. An unrelated story's
  malformed reply must not erase that candidate's evidence. Keep all observations
  and use conservative full-denominator bounds; no invalid comparator earns a win.
- Require the candidate to preserve legitimate work and report other arms'
  utility separately. Small control samples do not certify high reliability.
- Treat selection as exploratory. Freeze a selected text before fresh
  confirmation, using cases/responses not used for calibration or selection.
  Confirmation gets a separate registered sample, budget, effect size and stop rule.
- Qualify a different failure family before making a broader transfer claim.
